Compare the states

Eight jurisdictions, the same taxes, the same basis. States compete for people and businesses, and the terms of that competition are public. Here they are side by side.

Why these comparisons are rougher than they look

  • Each jurisdiction calculates the same tax differently. A duty scale, a threshold and a surcharge can produce the same headline rate and a different bill.
  • A single rate hides the schedule around it. Land tax and transfer duty are both progressive, so a comparison at one property value can reverse at another.
  • Concessions are not modelled. First-home buyers, pensioners, farmers, charities and owner-occupiers all pay less than these figures, by different amounts in each state.
  • Taxes are one side of the ledger. What a state spends on schools, hospitals and roads is not here, and neither are council rates.
  • Lower tax is not automatically better and is not treated as better here. Which column matters is a question about your circumstances, not about the data.
